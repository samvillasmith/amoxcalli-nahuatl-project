Flor y Canto Nahuatl now converges on two deliverables: a validated Modern Standard Nahuatl database, and Learn Modern Standard Nahuatl, the learner book that teaches MSN for reading, writing, prose, and poetry while preserving register discipline.

Start Here

Database · Validation · Learner book

The project has one practical convergence point: a validated Modern Standard Nahuatl database and the final learner book, Learn Modern Standard Nahuatl. The database is the source of truth for forms, registers, source notes, examples, grammar decisions, and validation status. The book is the public synthesis that teaches people to read, write, publish, and compose in MSN responsibly.

For Reviewers & Linguists

Validate the system, not a sample.

Reviewers are being asked to validate the whole MSN standard: orthography, register boundaries, grammar, headwords, examples, prose readiness, poetic readiness, and learner-book readiness. The reviewer packet explains what we built, how we got here, and what decisions are needed.

For Nahuatl Speakers & Learners

The end product is a book you can learn from.

The final book, Learn Modern Standard Nahuatl, should teach MSN as a usable written language: how to read it, write paragraphs, write short books or prose, understand register labels, and begin poetry or literary work without blurring MSN with spoken EHN or classical citation forms.

The Three-Layer Framework

Speech · Writing · Literature

Spoken Foundation

EHN

Eastern Huasteca Nahuatl. The living spoken base drawn from community speech. All pronunciation, phonology, and conversational register grounded here.

Written Standard

MSN

Modern Standard Nahuatl. The neutral written reference norm for education, publishing, governance, and formal communication. Clear, consistent, teachable.

Poetic Register

MSN-P

The elevated literary register for poetry, song, ceremony, and public oratory. Classical resonance with modern clarity. Where the language creates, not just communicates.

The instructional pipeline turns EHN lesson evidence into staged learner material and MSN-facing primer infrastructure. It keeps spoken instruction, written standardization, and register labeling connected instead of letting them drift apart.

Its practical role is to feed Learn Modern Standard Nahuatl: sequence lessons, select public-safe vocabulary, generate exercises, and support examples that are validated against the source-of-truth database.

A1 — Beginner

Alphabet, greetings, questions, names, numbers, colors, basic verbs, possessives, diminutives, household vocabulary, food, fields, conditional, non-specific objects.

A2 — Elementary

Professions, past tense (three parts), city navigation, colors and numbers in context. Building toward independent sentence production.

B1–B2 — Intermediate

Intransitive and transitive verbs, time division, family, appearance, reflexives, likes/dislikes, imperatives, market language, illness vocabulary, cleansing ceremonies.
